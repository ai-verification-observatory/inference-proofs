What one proof establishes

YesPartlyNoNot statedDraft criteria, needs an RFC →

  1. Weights hidden

    Does the checker learn nothing about the weights?

    Partly

    Hidden by design, but one bit per request leaks.

    “One bit per request is revealed (served or refused: a request is refused if it is ill-formed, if the model has no result on it, or if its result is other tokens), and it depends on the weights.”
    guide/what-is-proved.md L18-L20 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes
    • “Zero knowledge, Lean's form of weight hiding, is proved in Lean for the protocol, at full size and at the exact-match policy, with fresh random masks.”
      guide/weight-hiding.md L3 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

    • Also stated, and counted under Model bound only (the clipped-gap policy):

      “Lean's theorem on zero knowledge is for exact match: under a clipped-gap policy no Lean theorem covers what a whole proof reveals”
      guide/weight-hiding.md L41 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

  2. Model bound

    Is each checked token tied to the committed weights?

    Partly

    Only under exact match; the expected use checks just the client's limits.

    “WITHIN LIMITS says only that the checked tokens' costs together meet the limits that the client named, not that the reply is the model's.”
    guide/how-it-works.md L72-L73 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes · Our note
    • “In the expected use, an accepting check ends in WITHIN LIMITS, which says much less than YES.”
      guide/how-it-works.md L3-L5 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    • “Under exact match, a YES is meant to show that each checked token is the committed integer model's top choice at its position, up to the error bound.”
      guide/how-it-works.md L57-L59 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

    • Which real model: 'A yes does not say whose weights they are, that the shipped committer made the commitment, or which real model it is.' (guide/known-limits.md L29-L30). If the benchmark's FlashProve configuration fixes exact match (issue #69), this segment would be green for that configuration.

  3. Whole output proved

    Does one check cover every output token of a request?

    Partly

    Covers the positions the client names; completeness of the reply not shown.

    “A pass covers only checked tokens. Only the named output positions are covered, and a reply stopped short verifies as a shorter one.”
    guide/known-limits.md L51-L52 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes · Our note
    • “An accepted proof does not show that the provider committed the advertised model or a sensible one, or that the reply is complete.”
      guide/how-it-works.md L52-L53 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

    • The benchmark's FlashProve workloads prove the next token after a 1,024-token window (workloads/fp-maverick-w1024-b24.yaml).

  4. Formally verified

    Is the security argument checked by a proof assistant?

    Yes

    Soundness and zero knowledge proved in Lean.

    “The protocol's soundness is proved in Lean 4.”
    README.md L26 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes

    • Shared by every row and printed as text (programs tested, not proved):

      “The C++ verifier and the CUDA prover are written to follow the same protocol and are tested against it, not themselves proved.”
      guide/what-is-proved.md L7-L8 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

  5. Security level

    Is a soundness level stated, and proven?

    Yes

    Bound stated and proved in Lean for the protocol, in the random-oracle model.

    “The protocol's soundness is proved in Lean 4. A provider that tries to pass off output that is not the committed model's is caught, except with probability about q/2^124 + 2^-101 after q hash queries.”
    README.md L26-L28 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes · Our note

    • For the programs the benchmark runs, FlashProve states:

      “Neither figure, 124 bits per query or 128 bits per round, is claimed for the C++ programs.”
      guide/what-is-proved.md L70 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
      “The C++ programs' printed "bits" are their own accounting, not a theorem”
      guide/known-limits.md L65-L66 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

    • The bound is stated for exact-match claims and one proof; the clipped-gap limit is counted under Model bound only.

  6. No trusted party

    Does a check hold without trusting a chip maker, a setup ceremony or an honest party?

    Partly

    You must grant its public files were fixed before the hash.

    “No file shows that they were fixed before the provider could tune them to the hash function: that you grant.”
    guide/what-is-proved.md L288-L289 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    More quotes · Our note
    • “Configuration files fixed before the hash function is drawn: the three public files (parameter, layout and scale file). A provider that tunes them to the real hash function is outside the statement.”
      guide/what-is-proved.md L59-L61 · 9e2bdd8f ↗
    • “The random-oracle model for the hash, with a second, fixed hash function, built on BLAKE2b's compression function, assumed independent of it, against classical adversaries.”
      guide/what-is-proved.md L57-L58 · 9e2bdd8f ↗

    • Also stated and not counted here (shared by every row): 'The client must trust what no call checks: the commitment ID, the expected policy and its numbers, the tokenizer and chat template, and `cp_verify` itself.' (guide/how-it-works.md L39-L40); 'compilers and hardware are trusted' (guide/what-is-proved.md L277).

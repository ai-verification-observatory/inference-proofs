    Glyph

    Unforgeable

    green · in full
    The publisher states a proved soundness bound against any prover (arbitrary or computationally bounded) showing that a false reply is accepted with probability at most 2^-128 per term (where a term 2^-t or q/2^t has exponent t at least 128). The bound must cover the actual benchmark run: one proof per reply (or a batch bound that holds for each member checked on its own), using the row's parameters, with its preconditions satisfied by the row's files, and with the verifier binary we run proved or stated to implement the verifier the bound analyses.
    amber · in part
    Every condition for green holds, except the smallest exponent in the proved bound sits between 100 and 127 bits instead of reaching 128.
    amber · in lesser part
    The publisher states a bound whose exponents are at least 100 bits on a conjectured (or unstated) basis, or states a proved bound of at least 80 bits that either has an exponent below 100 or does not fully cover the run. For example, a proved bound lands here if the adversary model is left unstated, if the bound is given per layer or per round rather than per reply, if a proof covers multiple replies or only holds when a whole batch is checked together, if the parameters or preconditions are not shown for the row's files, or if the verifier binary is not stated to implement the protocol analysed.
    red · no
    The publisher states no soundness bound, assumes a restricted prover, states a proved bound with an exponent below 80 bits, states a conjectured (or unspecified) bound with an exponent below 100 bits, or our Model, Prompt, or Reply check failed.

    Weights hidden

    green · in full
    Our Verify without weights check held, the publisher states a proved zero-knowledge theorem for the configuration the row runs, the prover and verifier binaries are proved or stated to implement that protocol, and the publisher lists everything the proof and statement leave visible, with none of those visible values fitted to the model weights.
    amber · in part
    Our Verify without weights check held and the publisher states a proved zero-knowledge theorem, but the theorem covers only the abstract protocol, the binaries are only expected (or not stated) to implement it, the publisher does not list what remains visible, or the visible parameters include values fitted to the weights (such as quantisation scales).
    amber · in lesser part
    Our Verify without weights check held and the publisher states that the model weights remain hidden, but cites no proved zero-knowledge theorem.
    red · no
    The verifier requires the model weights, the publisher states that weights are not hidden (or says nothing about weight secrecy), or our Verify without weights check failed.

    Faithful model

    green · in full
    In a single-request-at-a-time fidelity run, the system's proved replies score significantly above the midpoint between a second run of the publisher's unquantised model and its 8-bit quantised copy (Q8_0).
    amber · in part
    The proved replies score significantly above the midpoint between the 8-bit (Q8_0) and 4-bit (Q4_K_M) quantised copies, or they clear the upper (green) midpoint in a batched fidelity run of more than one request at a time.
    amber · in lesser part
    Our Fidelity check held against the damaged calibration bar, but the replies did not clear the midpoint between Q8_0 and Q4_K_M, the system's claim card allows tokens other than the model's top token, or one of the reference or quantised controls is missing or failed.
    red · no
    Our Fidelity check failed.

    Machine-checked

    green · in full
    A machine-checked soundness theorem covers the actual verifier program the row runs, from the raw bytes it reads to the verdict it returns, with its trusted base explicitly listed and no unverified input-translation code outside the theorem, and we have recorded a clean build of that proof at the pinned commit using the publisher's pinned toolchain.
    amber · in part
    Our recorded build of the formal proof succeeded, and the theorem covers the full protocol and the statement encoding the model, but it does not cover the executable verifier program itself, leaves out the list of axioms and trusted base, or relies on unverified code to translate the model into the theorem's input.
    amber · in lesser part
    Our recorded build of the formal proof succeeded, but the theorem covers only a component of the system (such as a primitive or gadget), or covers only the abstract protocol or model statement in isolation.
    red · no
    The publisher states no machine-checked proof, our recorded build failed or reported axioms different from those declared, or a theorem that would have been green was lowered because our Model, Prompt, or Reply check failed.

    No third party trusted

    green · in full
    The publisher's documentation shows that soundness rests on standard cryptographic assumptions alone, with no setup step that ever generated a secret. Public parameters fixed before hashing or derived deterministically from public seeds qualify here.
    amber · in part
    Setup generated a secret through an open multi-party ceremony with a publicly verifiable transcript, where the secret remains unknown as long as at least one participant was honest.
    amber · in lesser part
    Verification relies on a ceremony among a fixed set of parties, an optimistic challenge window requiring an active challenger, or interactive or designated-verifier proofs that cannot be checked by arbitrary third parties.
    red · no
    Verification relies on a setup run by a single party, a hardware vendor's attestation key, another trusted key, or the publisher's documentation does not state what setup trust is required.

    The five petals of the glyph summarise what kind of proof the system produces: four report what the publisher's documentation states at the pinned commit, and Faithful model reports how closely the proved replies match the original weights in our fidelity suite. A passing check never raises a petal above what the publisher states.

    A petal is drawn as a hollow outline in the muted ink while it is still undecided: either we have not yet recorded the system's properties file at the pinned commit, its fidelity run has not finished, or (for Machine-checked) we have not yet run a clean build of the cited formal proof. Silence in the publisher's documentation never makes a petal hollow: if the documents at the pinned commit do not state a property, the petal is red with an open outline.

    not stated
    Red (open outline): the publisher's documentation at the pinned commit does not address this property. Its name is not struck through.
    stated, short of amber
    Red (filled): the publisher's documentation states that this property does not hold, or states parameters that fall below the minimum threshold for amber.
    our check failed
    Red (filled): one of our own verification checks failed, or our clean build of the publisher's formal proof failed or reported axioms different from those declared. The petal's row names which check failed.
    not read yet
    Hollow: we have not yet finished reading and recording the publisher's documentation for the commit this row runs.
    our check undecided
    Hollow: this petal depends on a benchmark measurement whose check is still undecided or has not been recorded yet.
    no proof build yet
    Hollow: the publisher documents a formal proof that would qualify for amber or green on Machine-checked, but we have not yet recorded a clean build of that theorem at the pinned commit and toolchain.

    Our reading: the step rests on our interpretation of a passage in the publisher's documentation rather than an explicit sentence stating the fact directly. We print the publisher's quotes beside our reading.

    The six checks

    The six checks are empirical tests we run on every system, on the same request (512 prompt tokens, 512 reply tokens, greedy, every reply token checked). A system passes on a model only when all six checks hold there.

    These six checks run each system's own prover and verifier from the outside. They do not attempt to cryptographically forge a proof or extract hidden weights: mathematical soundness and weight secrecy are reported from the publisher's documentation, not proved by our test suite.

    Run

    held
    Across all 3 sessions (each on its own prompt set), the system returned an accepted proof for every request, running the published code at its pinned commit without patches or proof-weakening flags.
    failed
    At least one prompt set failed to finish within 5 attempts, the system's own code rejected the request, or a counted session ran with a flag that weakens the proof.

    Model

    held
    When we modified a single model weight and generated a proof, the verifier rejected it; when we took an honest proof and swapped the weight commitment in the verifier's statement, the verifier rejected that too (3 of 3 trials each).
    failed
    The verifier accepted a tampered trial: it accepted a proof for weights other than the committed model.

    Prompt

    held
    When we changed a single prompt token in the statement given to the verifier, the verifier rejected the proof in 3 of 3 trials.
    failed
    The verifier accepted a tampered trial: it accepted a proof for a prompt other than the one that was proved.

    Reply

    held
    Every accepted proof covers all 512 reply tokens, and when we altered the first token, the last token, or a randomly chosen interior token in the verifier's statement, the verifier rejected the proof in 3 of 3 trials each.
    failed
    A proof covered fewer reply tokens than requested, or the verifier accepted a tampered trial with an altered reply token.

    Verify without weights

    held
    Every counted verification ran on a CPU machine with no GPU attached and no model weight file on disk, and our trace of every file the verifier opened confirmed it never read a weight file.
    failed
    The file-access trace showed the verifier opening a model weight file, or a verify call required one on disk.

    Fidelity

    held
    On our fidelity prompt suite, the system's proved replies matched the publisher's unquantised reference weights for more leading tokens than our calibration bar did. That bar is the highest-scoring intentionally damaged copy of the model (chosen from damaged copies that every honest quantisation still outperforms). Passing this check is a floor against broken weights; the Faithful model petal grades how close the replies actually are.
    failed
    The calibration bar scored above the system's replies: the proved replies matched the publisher's original weights for fewer leading tokens than an intentionally damaged copy of the model.

    Undecided

    system limit
    The system does not support what this test requires (the publisher's own statement is quoted on the system page).
    not run
    This check has not been run yet.
    not resolved
    The test ran but did not reach a conclusive verdict.
    not reproduced
    The verifier accepted a tampered input, and we are reproducing that with the system's own command line, outside our harness, before marking the check failed.

    Figures

    Cost vs serving

    m
    Steady-state GPU-seconds per proved reply after the first release group. Within a session, proofs released within 1 s of the previous proof form a release group. We measure the GPU time from the end of the first group to the final proof of the session and divide by the number of replies in those later groups (including token generation). Reported as the mean across 3 sessions.
    serving
    Baseline GPU-seconds per reply (b) for plain unproved serving: we run the publisher's original model weights under the pinned inference engine on the fewest H200 GPUs that fit the model, at full load. Full load is the lowest concurrency that reaches 95 % of peak throughput on a concurrency ladder up to 1,024 requests.
    multiple
    The ratio m / b on the same model. Because both numbers are measured in GPU-seconds on the same GPU type at the same hourly rate, this ratio is identical in dollars.
    whole session
    If a system's sessions release too few groups (or groups that are too uneven) to establish a steady state, it has no m. Instead, we divide its total session GPU time per reply by b and label the figure as whole sessions.
    parts
    When a system runs token generation in a separate phase from proving, we split the multiple into two parts (each divided by b): generation and the remaining work. For steady-state rows that remainder is proving; for whole-session rows it combines model commitment and proving.

    Cost per reply

    steady
    Steady-state GPU time per reply (m) converted to US dollars at the release's benchmark price for one H200 GPU-hour.
    session
    For systems without a steady-state multiple: total GPU time from the start of model commitment to the release of the last proof, divided by the session's replies, in US dollars.

    Verify time per reply

    verify
    Total CPU wall-clock time the system's verifier takes to check all proofs in a session, divided by the number of replies in that session.

    Proof size per reply

    size
    Total size of the proofs a session stores (every file of each proof, in bytes as stored), divided by the number of replies in that session. A proof that covers several replies is counted once. Reported as the mean across 3 sessions, in MB (one MB is 1,000,000 bytes).

    KL to the original weights

    kl
    Mean Kullback-Leibler divergence per scored token (in nats) between the model file the system commits to and the publisher's original reference weights (16‑bit for Llama 4 Maverick 17B-128E Instruct and Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, 32‑bit for GPT-2).
    computed by
    Measured directly on the committed weight file using llama.cpp rather than inside the prover. We print this figure for context; it does not decide any check.
    none
    Omitted when llama.cpp cannot load the format of the committed weight file.

    Order

    ranked
    Only systems that pass all six checks on a model are eligible to be ranked, and they are ranked only within that model's table.
    above
    One system is placed strictly above another when its cost multiple is at least 10 % lower and its slowest session (highest session m) is still faster than the other's fastest session (lowest session m). Systems with a steady-state multiple are placed above systems measured only on whole sessions; two whole-session systems are compared the same way using session cost per reply.
    position
    A system's position is 1 plus the number of systems placed strictly above it, shown alongside the total number of systems run on that model.
    unranked
    A system that passes all six checks is given a numbered position only when its publisher states a cryptographic security level at the pinned commit; otherwise it is listed as passed, unranked.
    glyph
    The five-petal glyph never affects a system's position in the table.
    models
    Nothing is ranked across models.

    For policy readers

    For people who decide whether a claim about an AI system can be relied on: analysts, advisers, regulators, auditors. No cryptography needed.

    1. 01Why verifyWhat it means to verify a claim about an AI system, why that is hard, and which part of it this site covers.
    2. 02What a proof of inference isA file that lets you check that a model fixed in advance gives a certain reply to a certain prompt, without rerunning the model and without seeing it. What a yes means, and what it does not.
    3. 03Three ways to checkRerun the computation, trust the hardware, or check a proof. What each asks you to trust, and what it costs.
    4. 04A treaty, stage by stageHow proofs of inference fit an agreement between two states that will not show each other their weights, stage by stage.
    5. 05Before you rely on a proofNine questions to put to any system that offers a proof, and where this site answers them.

    For engineers

    For people who build, run or test these systems: ML engineers new to proof systems, and cryptographers new to inference.

    1. 01The statementWhich sentence a proof proves, about which model, for which prompt and reply, in two systems' own words.
    2. 02Inference, for cryptographersWhat a language model computes when it answers, how it is served, and why the computation is awkward to prove.
    3. 03Proof systems, for ML engineersHow a short random check can stand in for rerunning a long computation: the ideas the provers on this site are built from.
    4. 04Proving a forward passHow a transformer's forward pass becomes something a verifier can check: matrix products by sumcheck, the other steps by lookups, the weights by a commitment.
    5. 05Design familiesThe ways to write the computation down and to commit to the weights, and the checks that are not proofs: what each makes cheap, what it makes expensive and what it has to trust, in their publishers' own words.
    6. 06Where the cost goesWhy a proved reply costs a multiple of a served one, and how that multiple is computed here.
    7. 07How we compareOne request, six checks, one ranked figure, and the reason for each rule, where one is given.

# Depth: the site is an iceberg

Read this guide before adding a line to any page. RFC-0018 SI-8 sets the rule; this document maps how the site applies it.

Readers move through the site from the top down. The surface presents the results, and each step deeper reveals more about how those results were obtained for readers who want to look closer. Someone at the surface knows nothing about the methodology and should not have to learn it just to read the table. Someone three steps down already understands what the site does and wants the exact details.

Place every piece of information by asking a single question: how deep is the reader who needs it? What a figure represents belongs near the top; how, when, and across how many runs it was measured belongs deeper. Nothing gets deleted to keep a page light. Instead, detail moves to its proper depth and remains one click away from where it started.

## The depths

| Depth | Where | The reader already knows | The question they are asking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Glance | share cards, link previews, page titles, and descriptions | nothing | What is this, and where does this system stand? |
| 1. Surface | the ladder (home) and the Overview page | that this is a table of systems that prove a model's reply | Can this model be proved, how large is it, did the system pass, and at what cost? |
| 2. System, top | a system's page: its top band (the Figures row was removed on 2026-11-17 so each figure appears once, in Cost or Fidelity) | one system on one model, along with its result | What did this system achieve, and what holds it back? |
| 3. System, body | a system's page: Criteria (titled Glyph until 2026-11-22), Statement, Checks, Cost, Fidelity, and Sessions | the result and the plain terms for it | How was each state and figure produced, and which record backs it? |
| 4. Rules | Checks, Mandate, and the glossary | what was measured | What does the rule say, who set it, and who runs this project? |
| 5. Records | the repository: board file, run records, level file, and RFCs | the rule | Can I recompute this myself? |

Two parts of the site sit alongside these depths rather than inside them:

- **Learn** offers an entry point for readers who want the subject explained from scratch rather than acting as a step between the ladder and a system page. Its hub also lists the mandate (its belief and its sections) and the glossary, two pages of depth 4 that stand under the Learn tab. Its pages follow two tracks (a policy track, and a track for prover teams and engineers). Because readers often open a Learn page on its own, any figure shown there carries its labels. One page, "How a check works", illustrates the request, who holds what, the six checks, and the headline fraction; both the home lead and the Checks page link to it.
- **The footer** stays identical across every page. It contains names only (the level, the release with its date, the licence, and who builds the site), each as a link: the level links to its entry on the Mandate page, the release links to its board file, and the licence links to its text in the repository. It never carries rule dates or hashes.

## Three laws

1. **Down, never out.** Content moves to its appropriate depth rather than being deleted. Each layer links directly to the next: a ladder row links to its system page, a ladder figure links to the relevant section on that page (the multiple, its two rates, proof size, and verify time link to Cost; Fidelity links to Fidelity), a term links to its definition, a state links to its evidence, and an evidence line links to its underlying record.
2. **A bad result never moves down.** "Failed", "not decided", "Not passed yet", "not measured", and "not run" stay visible at the surface in plain words beside the system they apply to. Only the detailed explanation of the result moves deeper.
3. **A hover title is a shaft, not a home.** A figure's hover title carries its labels (scope, n, grade, tier, and record) at any depth, but it is never the sole place those labels appear. Phones and keyboards do not have hover states, so the same labels always appear in visible text one step down.

## Words follow depth

- **The surface names the property; deeper layers name the measure.** Depths 0 to 2 use plain labels: "Fidelity", "Proof size", and "Verify time" ("Cost" was also here until 2026-11-16, when USD moved to depth 3). Exact metric names ("leading tokens matching the original, mean", "KL to the original weights") first appear at depth 3 as column headings, and the surface label links down to them.
- **The heading says what; the unit says per what.** Write "Cost" beside `USD/reply`, never "Cost per reply" beside `USD/reply`. `FIGURE_HEAD` in `draw/words.py` stores these headings, and `beside_unit` trims longer names whenever a unit follows. Names printed without a unit (such as definition headings or prose mentions) keep their full form.
- **Once per block.** Anything shared by all figures in a block (scope, n, model, or date) is stated once for the entire block rather than repeated on each figure. Only a figure that differs from its block states its own exception.
- **No bare fraction.** Never frame a figure as "of N" unless N is what the figure is evaluated against. Because the mean matched prefix is judged against the model's bar rather than the full reply length, it is printed alongside the bar or not at all.
- **A state keeps its word at every depth.** Use `passed`, `failed`, and `not decided` for checks; `Met`, `Mostly met`, `Partly met`, `Not met`, `Not stated`, `Failed`, and `Not decided` for petals; and `Passed`, `Not passed`, and `Not passed yet` for rows. Never use colour names as state words: the visual ink communicates colour, and summarizing at a shallower depth never softens a state.
- **No visible ID above depth 3.** Rule IDs, file names, and control IDs appear only in hover titles above depth 3. The surface says "8-bit copy", while its hover title and the Fidelity table say `Q8_0`.
- **Shown, not told** (2026-11-23, RFC-0018 Q54). What the layout already shows is not also printed beside it. The ladder prints no badge for the largest model with a passed row (it runs largest model first, and nothing replaces the badge). It prints no count beside a position's square (the rows under the divider are the count), no "Passed" beside a square on a wide screen, no "proved" on the rate under the multiple (it stands under the served rate), and no head over the square, the glyph or the name; a screen reader hears each of these. A head names its column in the site's term, linked to its definition, with the plain question it answers in its hover title. A bad, missing or undecided result is never left to the layout: its words stay on screen.
- **One name, two things, two homes.** Fidelity refers to both a figure and a check. The surface prints the figure, while the check is one of the six entry gates. Where both appear together (in a system's Fidelity block), each is clearly labelled ("Fidelity" heads the plot, "Fidelity check" heads its own cell) and links to its own definition on the Checks page.

## The test

Before placing a new line, ask these questions in order; the first "yes" decides its depth:

1. Would moving it deeper hide a failure, an undecided state, an unmeasured figure, or a missing run? Keep it at depth 0 or 1 as visible text.
2. Does a reader who only knows the benchmark exists need it to see which system passed on which model, and at what cost? Place it at depth 0 or 1.
3. Does it state what one specific system achieved, or what holds it back? Place it at depth 2.
4. Does it explain how, when, or across how many runs something was measured, or quote a source? Place it at depth 3, and put a summary in the figure's hover title above that.
5. Does it apply to every system (a rule, threshold, definition, operator detail, or rationale)? Place it at depth 4 and link to it where relevant. Is it an internal ID, file path, or hash? Place it at depth 5 behind a link.

Finally, before printing anything, check whether a heading, unit, glyph, or another block on the same page already states it. If so, link there instead of repeating it.

## Exceptions, each deliberate

- **The proving part at the surface** (2026-11-26, RFC-0018 CO-9, Q55, releases after v1.0). The proving multiple stands beside the total at depths 0 to 2 because the pair reads the two ways a provider can use a system (letting it write the reply, or proving a reply served elsewhere); a row the board does not split says "not measured" there.
- **The two parts of the multiple** (withdrawn 2026-11-17, RFC-0018 Q47). These previously appeared on the ladder, the band, the Overview page, and share cards, one depth higher than the test recommends (CO-7, Q15). Because not every system times generation and proving separately, they now sit at their natural depth under Cost on the system page.
- **The two rates on the ladder** (amended 2026-11-16, RFC-0018 CO-8, Q39). Hardware-bound figures normally belong deeper, but readers consistently ask for tokens per second. Because a raw rate depends on the GPU, it appears only as the numerator and denominator behind the multiple on a single GPU type with the GPU named: the served rate on the model's divider, and the proved rate on one line under the multiple, in the same column (amended 2026-11-17, 2026-11-23).
- **The overview lead** (RFC-0018 SI-1, amended 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16). Definitions usually live deeper, but a first-time reader arrives at the surface with zero background. The top band therefore explains what a proof of inference is in one sentence and links to "How a check works". The extra sentence explaining what "passed" means was removed on 2026-11-16 (Q39) so the models start higher up the page; "Passed" on each row links directly to its definition on the Checks page.
- **The two reference copies travelling with Fidelity.** A raw percentage of matching tokens has no intuitive scale: a score in the eighties looks broken until you see that a standard 4-bit copy of the same model scores roughly the same. The 8-bit and 4-bit copies provide that reference scale (RFC-0018 SI-9). On the ladder they appear once per model, on its divider, over its rows' Fidelity figures, in smaller type with their names in the label's ink, so they read as the model's scale and not as a row's own figures (amended 2026-11-23, Q54); on a system page they stand beside the figure in the Fidelity block's plot.
- **The release and date on a share card.** Because a share card circulates outside the live page it came from, the release name and date tell readers which snapshot the numbers reflect.

## Known gaps

## Decisions

- 2026-11-17, owner decision (RFC-0018 Q44): each petal on a system page states what Met needs, what the system has, what it misses and who acts, and where to verify those facts, all in plain language generated from the board. A short lead above the petals defines the terms once. Default rows stay compact so all five petals fit on one desktop screen; exact facts and publisher quotes sit one click down, and the rule engine's raw strings (`reason` and scale label) remain on the board at depth 5.
- 2026-11-23, owner decision: the partners stand at the bottom of the home page with their marks, after the systems not run, so the board above them is read first; "convened by" replaces "led by" for the organisation that convenes the project (footer, Mandate, the members' card).
- 2026-11-17, owner decision (Q45, refined after three judged layouts): the glyph's grade strip is explicitly labelled and marks the grade order; "Met needs" and "Has" stay visible beneath a petal's summary sentence when expanded; the expanded panel shows Missing beside the evidence; check states on system pages appear as coloured text words just like grades; and every disclosure across a system page uses the same plain boxless toggle with a label and sign.
- 2026-11-17, owner decision: every section below the glyph on a system page follows the same pattern as the ladder, showing what was computed, what it means, and what it says for this system. Figures come first with their units, shared metadata sits in a single line above them, and prose is limited to at most one sentence. Explanations of measurement methods and rule rationales live on the Checks page and in Learn, linked from headings or terms. Detailed system data (raw records, individual sessions, all control copies, and publisher quotes) sits one click down inside one native disclosure per block.
- 2026-11-17, owner decision (applied across the system page after the plain-petals review): every figure appears exactly once. The Figures row was removed (verify time and proof size moved under Cost with n and range, and the Fidelity figure moved under Fidelity), and the Cost block stopped repeating the band's multiple and its parts. Expanded petal panels and "Why this grade" show only facts about that specific system; all general thresholds, rules, and open questions moved to the Checks page under the petal steps.
- 2026-11-17, owner decision (RFC-0018 Q47, amended 2026-11-17): the two parts of the multiple (generation, and commit and proving) appear only under Cost on a system page because not every system times those phases separately, making them unsuitable as surface figures. The ladder cell shows the multiple followed by the proved rate line; the band, the Overview page, share cards, page descriptions, and search entries show the multiple alone.
- 2026-11-19, owner request (RFC-0018 Q50): include a chart only when a figure has something concrete to compare against, and nowhere else. Three alternative chart sets were built on real pages and reviewed by a cold reader for rule compliance and craft. Kept: the two rates on a shared decade axis in their own unit, the multiple's two parts as a single split bar, the Fidelity figure among the quantized copies on one axis, and the serving ladder bars. Every chart sits alongside its figure without repeating it; bars start at zero; logarithmic axes mark positions only and explicitly label themselves; in both dot plots the system's mark is solid paper and reference marks are outlines; and neither state inks nor cobalt are used to encode data values.
